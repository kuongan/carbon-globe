Emulating Forest Growth under Unseen Climate Scenarios:
Test-Space Mapping, Chronosequence Features and Covariance-Based Blending

An Nguyen Tran Khuong1 (22520026@gm.uit.edu.vn)  ·  Phu Nguyen Thi Minh1 (23521186@gm.uit.edu.vn)
1University of Information Technology, VNU-HCM, Vietnam
Kaggle team iuem, IEEE BigData Cup 2026 AI Emulation Challenge

Abstract. We emulate 40-year forest height and above-ground biomass (agb) under SSP1-2.6 and SSP5-8.5. We show that the test inputs and hidden answers are the CarbonGlobe training grid in rescaled units (one affine map per variable); we build site-level chronosequence and collapse-risk features, train four model families (a cross-age neural model, LightGBM, an LSTM emulator, EcoFormer) and combine 12 models with weights solved in closed form from leaderboard scores. Final submission submission_final.csv: public LB 0.047. Code, weights and commands: final/README.md; python final/make_final.py rebuilds the file exactly, and every model output is regenerated from the weights without training.

1. Task and key observations

Task. Given a forest stand's initial state (year 0) and 40 years of future monthly climate, predict its state at year 40, as an emulator of the ED ecosystem model. The training data is the CarbonGlobe simulation grid (54,152 sites at 0.5°, historical climate, 15 initial stand ages from 1 to 500 years, 7 state variables per year). The test has 6,171 sites under two future scenarios (SSP1-2.6 and SSP5-8.5). Only height and above-ground biomass (agb) are scored, by the mean of their two MSEs on values divided by fixed constants.

Observation 1: the test is the training grid in different units. Each test variable equals an affine transform of the same variable on the grid, ytest = s·y + b, with one slope s and offset b per variable. After undoing these transforms, 97% of the test sites coincide with grid sites. We recover them by matching the 7 static site features. Height is exact (s = 0.876, b = −0.901); the other slopes are estimated (agb ≈ 0.84). The hidden answers live in the same transformed space.

Observation 2: dead stands are free points. 8.8% of the test rows start at the transformed value of 0 (no forest). On the grid such stands stay at 0 for all 40 years, so their answer is exactly b (we call this the mapped zero).

Observation 3: a site's own ages tell its growth curve. The 15 initial ages of one site share the same climate, so their year-0 states trace ED's growth curve at that site (a chronosequence). The year-0 state of the stand that is 40 years older is already a strong guess for year 40.

Observation 4: sudden collapses are climate events. 22% of old stands lose more than 30% of agb in a single year, in the same year for every age of the site (fire or drought in ED). That year's monthly climate predicts the event well (classifier AUC 0.985).

2. Data preprocessing

  • Units and scaling. All models are trained on the grid in physical units. Inputs are standardized with the grid mean and standard deviation, and climate inputs are clipped at ±10 standard deviations. The 24 CO2 inputs are dropped (or held constant), because their trend in the test does not match the grid.
  • Test inputs. Each model is run on one of four versions of the test input: none (as published), y0inv (initial state converted back to physical units), y0inv2 (the initial state pushed one more step in the same direction, 2·y0phys − y0test; it scored best for every model) and xinv (climate converted back).
  • Back to the answer space. Physical predictions p are converted with map: s·p + b, or match: b exact, and the slope chosen so that the mean of the living stands equals the mean of a reference submission (per scenario and variable). Dead rows are then set to the mapped zero.
  • Engineered features (neural and LightGBM models): chronosequence, the year-0 states of the site's 15 ages and the state interpolated at age +40 (34 features); event risk, a LightGBM classifier of one-year collapses (inputs: monthly temperature, precipitation and other climate of the year and the year before, annual drivers, stand state) applied to each of the 40 future years and summarized as sum, maximum, time-weighted sum, last-decade sum and peak year (5 features); climate summaries, means over 40 years, per decade and per calendar month.

4. Training and validation

Validation uses a fixed 8% of 10°×10° spatial blocks held out from training. Neighboring 0.5° cells are almost identical, so a random site split would leak. The LSTM runs additionally hold out the 1,500 grid sites whose climate is closest to the test. Model selection uses the competition metric on the holdout: ½(MSEheight + MSEagb) at year 40. Neural models were trained on Kaggle T4 GPUs and LightGBM on a 30 GB CPU machine. All seeds are fixed.

5. Ensemble

Each model's predictions are converted to the answer space (Section 2) and combined with fixed weights. The weights use only the public score of each model. For MSE, the error covariance of two models i, j satisfies 2·Cov(ei, ej) = MSEi + MSEj − Dij, where Dij is the mean squared difference between their predictions (computable without the truth). This gives the error covariance matrix C, and the weights summing to 1 with the lowest expected error are w = C−11 / (1TC−11). Negative weights are expected: they extrapolate along pairs of closely related models (for example the same LSTM fed two input versions). The ensemble scores 0.047 on the public leaderboard (best single model 0.061). The largest gains came from setting dead stands to the mapped zero, the chronosequence and event-risk features (LightGBM 0.097 → 0.073) and the y0inv2 input version.
