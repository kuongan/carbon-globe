Emulating Forest Growth under Unseen Climate Scenarios:
Test-Space Mapping, Chronosequence Features and Covariance-Based Blending

An Nguyen Tran Khuong1 (22520026@gm.uit.edu.vn)  ·  Phu Nguyen Thi Minh1 (23521186@gm.uit.edu.vn)
1University of Information Technology, VNU-HCM, Vietnam
Kaggle team iuem, IEEE BigData Cup 2026 AI Emulation Challenge

Abstract. We build a fast emulator of a forest simulator: from a stand's initial state and 40 years of future climate, it predicts forest height and above-ground biomass at year 40. Three findings drive the solution. First, the test data are the training simulations expressed in rescaled units, which lets us train in physical units and convert exactly. Second, the initial states of the same site at other stand ages reveal its growth curve. Third, sudden biomass collapses are tied to single climate years and can be forecast. A cross-age neural network built on these features reaches a public score of 0.061 (lower is better; provided baseline 0.231, no-change prediction 0.285). An ensemble of 12 models whose weights are solved in closed form from their public scores reaches 0.046.

1. Task and key observations

Task. The challenge asks for an emulator of ED (the Ecosystem Demography model [1]), a process-based simulator of forest dynamics: given a forest stand's state at year 0 and 40 years of future monthly climate, predict the stand at year 40. Training data is the CarbonGlobe simulation grid [2]: 54,152 land sites at 0.5°, historical climate (136 input features per month), stands started at 15 ages (1 to 500 years), and 7 state variables per year. The test has 6,171 sites under two future climate scenarios, SSP1-2.6 (low emissions) and SSP5-8.5 (high emissions). Only height and above-ground biomass (agb) at year 40 are scored: ½(MSEheight + MSEagb) on values divided by fixed constants (lower is better).

Observation 1: the test is stored in different units. Every test variable is an affine transform of the same quantity on the grid, ytest = s·y + b, with one slope s and offset b per variable; the hidden answers use the same transform. We found this by matching the 7 static site features (soil, location; not to be confused with the 7 state variables): after undoing the transforms, 97% of test sites coincide with a grid site. Height is exact (s = 0.876, b = −0.901; the ED cap of 35 m maps to the test maximum). Other slopes are fitted by regressing test year-0 states on the year-0 states of the matched grid site and age (agb: 0.84). The match is used only to estimate the transforms and for the analog model; all models predict every test site, matched or not.

Observation 2: dead stands are known. 8.8% of test rows start at b, the transformed value of 0 (no forest). On the grid such stands always stay at 0 for 40 years, so we set their answer to b (the mapped zero).

Observation 3: a site's own ages tell its growth curve. The 15 ages of one site share the climate, so their year-0 states trace ED's growth curve there (a chronosequence). The year-0 state of the stand 40 years older is already a strong guess for year 40.

Observation 4: collapses are climate events. 22% of old stands lose more than 30% of agb in a single year, in the same year for all ages of a site (fire or drought). That year's monthly climate predicts the event (AUC 0.985).

2. Data preprocessing

  • Inputs. Of the 136 input features, 24 form a CO2 block whose trend in the test does not match the grid and one is constant; both are dropped, leaving 111 drivers. Inputs are standardized with the grid mean and standard deviation and clipped at ±10 sd. Models are trained in physical units.
  • Engineered features (neural model and LightGBM). Chronosequence (34): year-0 height and agb of the site's 15 ages (30), plus height and agb interpolated at age +40 and their gaps to the stand's own year 0 (4). Event risk (5): a LightGBM classifier of one-year collapses (climate of the year and the year before, annual drivers, stand state) is applied to each of the 40 future years and summarized as sum, maximum, time-weighted sum, last-decade sum and peak year. Climate summaries: means over 40 years, per decade and per calendar month.
  • Feeding the test to a model trained in physical units. The test inputs must be converted. We tried four versions: as published (none); year-0 state converted to physical units (y0inv); climate converted (xinv); and y0inv2 = 2·y0phys − y0test, which moves the year-0 state one step past physical units. y0inv2 is an empirical heuristic: it scored best on the leaderboard for every model, and we have no physical explanation for it.

4. Training, validation and results

Validation holds out a fixed 8% of 10°×10° spatial blocks (4,170 sites), since neighboring 0.5° cells are near copies; the metric is the competition metric. Neural models were trained on Kaggle T4 GPUs, LightGBM on a 30 GB CPU machine; all seeds are fixed. The holdout ranks models correctly within a family but does not predict the leaderboard (test climate is future, the holdout's is historical), so final choices used the public leaderboard (LB).

5. Inference and ensemble

Inference. Each model predicts both scenarios from its input version (Section 2). Physical predictions p are converted to the answer units by map (s·p + b) or match: b exact and s chosen so the mean over living stands equals that of a fixed reference, our earlier scored submission (LB 0.072), calibrating the estimated agb slope. Dead stands are set to b.

Ensemble weights. For MSE, the errors of two models i, j satisfy 2·Cov(ei, ej) = MSEi + MSEj − Dij, where Dij is the mean squared difference of their predictions (no truth needed). With each model's public LB as MSE this gives the error covariance C, and the weights summing to 1 with the lowest expected error are w = C−11 / (1TC−11). Twelve of 63 scored models were chosen by greedy forward selection with swaps (Table 2). Negative weights extrapolate between related models.
